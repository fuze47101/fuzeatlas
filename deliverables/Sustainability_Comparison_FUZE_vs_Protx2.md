## ⚠ Major finding: IFTNA marketing contradicts their EPA registration

Before walking through the chemistry, the most significant finding from this analysis is a fundamental contradiction between what IFTNA tells customers and what IFTNA filed with the EPA.

**IFTNA's website (iftna.com/protx2):**
> "PROTX2® is a powerful breakthrough eco-friendly **zinc based technology**..."
> "PROTX2® is extremely durable and **does not contain nanosilver particles** that leach out of textiles during the manufacturing and the laundering process."
> "**Zinc is a naturally occurring element** that is safe for the environment..."

**EPA Master Label 87246-13 (filed by IFTNA, April 10, 2023) — the legally binding formulation:**
> Active Ingredients:
> 3-(Trimethoxysilyl)Propyldimethyloctadecyl ammonium chloride 0.65%
> Polyhexamethane Biguanide (PHMB) 0.26%
> **Silver 0.18%**
> Propiconazole 0.44%
> (Hydrolyzes to release approximately 0.1% methanol)
>
> **Zinc: 0%**

The EPA registration explicitly contains silver (the very chemistry the marketing denies). It does NOT contain zinc.

**This is a Federal Insecticide, Fungicide, and Rodenticide Act (FIFRA) issue.** The exact language from EPA's notification letter accompanying the PROTX2 registration:

> "claims made on the website may not substantially differ from those claims approved through the registration process. Therefore, should the Agency find or if it is brought to our attention that a website contains false or misleading statements or claims substantially differing from the EPA approved registration, the website will be referred to the EPA's Office of Enforcement and Assurance."

Per FIFRA Section 12(a)(1)(E), product claims that substantially differ from the approved registration constitute misbranding, and the product is "unlawful to sell or distribute."

**What this means for a brand considering PROTX2:**
A brand purchasing PROTX2 based on the website's "zinc-based, no nanosilver" positioning is buying a different chemistry than what's actually on their fabric. If that brand then markets their finished garment as containing IFTNA's "zinc-based antimicrobial" — based on PROTX2's own website — they are potentially propagating misbranding claims downstream, including in territories where silver-treated textiles face additional restrictions (California Prop 65, EU REACH biocides directive).

The question for the brand is direct: **which version are you actually getting on your fabric — the marketing version or the EPA-registered version?**

## What PROTX2® Actually Is

Per the EPA-registered product label (87246-13, dated April 10, 2023):

### Active ingredients (4 active chemicals + 1 hydrolysis byproduct):

| Chemical | Concentration | Role | Concern |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3-(Trimethoxysilyl)propyldimethyloctadecyl ammonium chloride | 0.65% | Silane-quat surface bonding + contact-kill | EU H315/H319 skin/eye irritant for QAC class; ES&T 2023 classified as chemical of emerging environmental concern |
| Polyhexamethane Biguanide (PHMB) | 0.26% | Broad-spectrum biocide | EU CLP H315/H319/H410 (skin irritant + very toxic to aquatic life with long-lasting effects) |
| Silver | 0.18% | Ion-release antimicrobial | Leaches independently of silane bond; aquatic toxicity, landfill leachate persistence |
| Propiconazole | 0.44% | Antifungal | EU CMR-3 (suspected endocrine disruptor), H410 very toxic to aquatic life |
| Methanol | ~0.1% (byproduct) | Released by hydrolysis of the silane | Neurotoxic VOC; released onto the fabric during application |

### Critical EPA restriction (verbatim from label)

> "It is prohibited to incorporate this product into textiles that may come into contact with skin, such as apparel, furnishings, and household fabrics (except shower curtains and window shades)."

**Allowed applications** (only):
- Shower curtains
- Window shades
- Carpet backing
- Inner layer of flooring
- Mat backings (not the mat surface)
- Conveyor belts (food processing)
- Cart covers (food handling)
- Canvas

**Explicitly NOT allowed:**
- Apparel
- Bedding (sheets, pillowcases, comforters, blankets)
- Towels
- Upholstery
- Carpet pile (only the BACKING is allowed)
- Any household fabric that contacts skin

This is a regulatory limitation, not a marketing choice. The EPA imposed it during registration because the stacked chemistry — particularly PHMB and propiconazole — was not approved for skin contact under FIFRA section 12.

## What FUZE Is

A single proprietary metamaterial active ingredient, bonded permanently into the fiber surface during standard textile finishing. Non-leaching. Contact-kill mechanism.

**No PHMB. No propiconazole. No methanol byproduct. No QAC. No silver-ion release.**

OEKO-TEX® Standard 100 Class I — the strictest skin-contact certification, valid for textiles intended for direct contact with infant skin. bluesign® approved. EPA-registered federal + California EPA approved (Q1 2026). PFAS-free.

Application surface: **every textile category** — apparel, bedding, towels, hospitality, healthcare, performance, athletic, childcare, infant.

## Where Each Chemistry Belongs

**PROTX2®** is genuinely good for the surfaces it's approved for. If a brand needs a backing layer for carpet or a window shade or a hospital cart cover where antimicrobial protection matters and human skin contact is impossible, PROTX2® has a legitimate role.

**FUZE** is engineered for the 90% of textile applications that DO involve skin contact — apparel, performance, hospitality bedding, healthcare scrubs, baby and childcare textiles, athletic gear, towels.

These are different products for different problems. A brand choosing an antimicrobial for an apparel line shouldn't be choosing between FUZE and PROTX2® — PROTX2® isn't eligible for that application under federal law.

## Comparison Detail by Sustainability Dimension

### Aquatic toxicity

| Active | EU CLP Hazard Statement | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| PROTX2® PHMB component | H410 — very toxic to aquatic life with long-lasting effects | Does not biodegrade in standard wastewater treatment |
| PROTX2® propiconazole component | H410 — very toxic to aquatic life with long-lasting effects | Half-life >180 days in soil; persistent organic |
| PROTX2® silver component | Silver aquatic toxicity (Daphnia, fish embryos) | Independent of silane bond; leaches separately |
| PROTX2® QAC silane component | Toxic to Daphnia magna at 5.8 μg/L | Classified as chemical of emerging concern (ES&T 2023) |
| FUZE | Non-leaching by design — metamaterial bonded permanently to fiber surface | Nothing released to wash water during normal use |

### End-of-life

PROTX2®: stacked-chemistry textile creates landfill / incineration complications across four separate active-chemistry classes. Disrupts biological wastewater treatment more severely than any single-active product.

FUZE: end-of-life textile carries non-leaching bonded metamaterial. No chemistry release during decomposition. Standard textile end-of-life recycling pathways apply.

### Skin exposure

PROTX2®: EPA-prohibited from skin-contact applications. PHMB component is a recognized skin irritant (EU H315/H319) and has been linked to contact dermatitis in concentration-dependent studies.

FUZE: certified for direct skin contact including infant skin (OEKO-TEX Standard 100 Class I).

### Testing scope

PROTX2®'s EPA-registered efficacy claim is limited to *Staphylococcus aureus* and *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*. The label does not certify performance against:
- *Klebsiella pneumoniae* (gram-negative, non-motile — the standard healthcare-pathogen counterpoint to *Staph*)
- *Moraxella osloensis* (the laundry-malodor causative agent that activewear brands care about)
- *Escherichia coli* (gram-negative motile — the standard ASTM E2149 organism)
- Antifungal challenge organisms (despite containing propiconazole, an antifungal)
- Antiviral challenge organisms

FUZE testing per the FUZE Certified Testing Protocol covers all six standard challenge organisms across ASTM E2149, AATCC 100, ISO 20743, JIS L 1902, AATCC 30 (antifungal), and ISO 18184 (antiviral).

### Wash count claim provenance

PROTX2®: 30 industrial washes is the manufacturer-published number. No third-party AATCC 100 wash-progression validation reports are publicly disclosed by IFTNA / iFabric Corp.

FUZE: F1 Full Spectrum tier is rated at 100 washes. Third-party AATCC 100 + ASTM E2149 wash-progression validation reports from accredited labs (Bureau Veritas, Intertek, ITS, Formosa Labs) are available to brands on request.

## How to Use This Comparison

This document is positioning collateral for sales conversations where a brand is comparing FUZE against PROTX2®. The asymmetry is genuine and significant: these products solve different problems and PROTX2® is regulated out of the majority of textile applications.

When a brand engineer or sustainability lead raises PROTX2®, the right opening question is: **"What's the end product? Apparel, bedding, or industrial backing?"** If it's apparel or bedding or towels or any skin-contact application, PROTX2® isn't legally eligible. If it's industrial backing, both products may be valid candidates — the comparison then shifts to wash durability, testing scope, ingredient transparency, and sustainability dimensions covered above.

Never speak ill of PROTX2® for the surfaces where it's appropriate. Speak factually about where it's restricted and what FUZE offers across the full textile category.
