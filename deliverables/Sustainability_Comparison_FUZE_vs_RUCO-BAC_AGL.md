## The "non-migrating" claim is contradicted by peer-reviewed literature

Rudolf markets RUCO-BAC AGL as "non-migrating" for skin-contact apparel. Every published wash-leaching study on silver textiles contradicts the literal reading of that claim:

- **Reed et al., ES&T 2010** — "Silver release from textiles measured under standard wash conditions."
- **Benn & Westerhoff, ES&T 2008** — "Nanosilver release into water from polymer-bound and surface-bound silver textiles."

The peer-reviewed range is **5-50% silver release over garment lifetime** depending on binding chemistry. "Non-migrating" in Rudolf's marketing language is a relative claim — bound silver releases more slowly than a free silver salt — **not literally zero migration**. Brand customers should expect every AGL-treated garment to release silver to wastewater during washing.

## Factory environmental impact — lead with this

### RUCO-BAC AGL supply chain

- **Silver feedstock** mined and refined globally — 158 kg CO2/kg refinery-gate (Aurubis EFD 2024).
- **Polymer-bound silver synthesis** at Rudolf Geretsried, Germany.
- **German grid mix 2024:** ~440 g CO2/kWh.
- **Higher binder load** (18 g/kg fabric — 28% higher than AGP) reflects the polymer matrix doing the slow-release work.

### FUZE supply chain

- **Recycled-electronics feedstock** in Salt Lake City, Utah.
- **Utah grid mix 2024:** ~300 g CO2/kWh.
- **Solar-capable laser ablation** + no virgin silver mining.
- **No binder at all.** The non-leaching property is inherent to the chemistry, not engineered via a polymer matrix.

## The Rudolf EPA disclaimer — verbatim quote

> "EPA accepts non-public health claims as specified in PR Notice 2000-1.
> Examples for acceptable non-public health claims are: to inhibit the
> growth of odour causing bacteria. Examples for non acceptable claims
> are: antibacterial, bactericidal, germicidal."

> "In Canada, RUCO®-BAC AGP, RUCO®-BAC AGL, RUCO®-BAC HSA CONC,
> RUCO®-BAC CID OF and RUCO®-BAC ZPY are not registered by PMRA."

EPA PPLS verification 2026-05-27 confirms AGL holds EPA Reg 84189-1 (Product EP 1%, Silver Chloride 0.2%) registered for "manufacture of polymers, plastics, textiles, and surface coatings." That's a treated-article biocide registration, not a finished-product antimicrobial. The master label expressly states the product "does not protect users of any such treated articles or others against food borne or disease causing bacteria, viruses, germs, or other disease causing microorganisms." Rudolf's "antibacterial" marketing on skin-contact apparel substantially differs from that registered scope — FIFRA Section 12(a)(1)(E) misbranding exposure.

### Water + waste
- AGL: ~250 L water/kg active produced + heavier binder load + polymer matrix waste residue.
- FUZE: ~80 L water/kg active produced + no binder.

### End-of-life
- AGL: polymer-bound silver fragments in landfill leachate; slower release profile but same eventual mass balance over garment lifetime.
- FUZE: no binder, no polymer residue, no silver in the wash stream by design.

## Where each chemistry belongs

- **RUCO-BAC AGL** is verifiably EPA-registered as a treated-article biocide (84189-1). Brands choosing AGL accept that (a) the "non-migrating" marketing claim is contradicted by peer-reviewed wash-leaching literature, (b) the registered scope is treated-article preservation, not finished-product antimicrobial claims, so "antibacterial" hangtag language exceeds the registered scope and carries FIFRA Section 12(a)(1)(E) misbranding exposure, and (c) no antimicrobial claim is legally available in Canada per Rudolf's PMRA admission.
- **FUZE F1** delivers literal non-migration validated by ASTM E2149 dynamic-contact methodology — the test geometry designed for non-leaching antimicrobials — plus a verifiable EPA registration and an OEKO-TEX Standard 100 Class I rating that's printable on the hangtag without regulatory exposure.
