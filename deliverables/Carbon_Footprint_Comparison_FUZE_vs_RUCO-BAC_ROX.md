# Carbon Footprint Comparison — FUZE vs RUCO-BAC ROX (AGXX)

**Published:** 2026-05-27
**Audience:** Brand sustainability lead evaluating Rudolf's premium silver-ruthenium catalytic finish
**Brands referenced:** Rudolf GmbH (Geretsried, Germany) · Heraeus Hanau (Germany) · FUZE Biotech (Salt Lake City, USA)
**Scope:** Cradle-to-gate CO2eq for the highest-CO2 antimicrobial textile finish in our competitive catalog

---

## Headline finding

**RUCO-BAC ROX is the highest-CO2 antimicrobial textile finish in our competitive catalog.** Per kg of active ingredient, AGXX (silver-ruthenium catalytic) carries approximately **763 kg CO2 vs FUZE metamaterial at <38 kg CO2/kg active — a 20× difference driven entirely by ruthenium mining.**

The math is single-variable: ruthenium has roughly **30× higher per-kg CO2 than silver** (5000 kg CO2/kg refinery-gate vs 158 for silver). At AGXX's typical 12.5% Ru / 87.5% Ag ratio:

| Component | Mass fraction | CO2 per kg | Weighted CO2 contribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Silver | 87.5% | 158 kg/kg | 138 kg CO2 / kg active |
| Ruthenium | 12.5% | 5000 kg/kg | 625 kg CO2 / kg active |
| **Weighted total active** | 100% | — | **763 kg CO2 / kg active** |

Even at 12.5% by mass, **ruthenium contributes 82% of the mining-stage CO2.**

---

## Why ruthenium is so carbon-heavy

Ruthenium is recovered as a **byproduct of platinum and palladium mining**, primarily from the **Bushveld Complex in South Africa** — the most CO2-intensive precious-metal mining region globally:

1. **South African grid is coal-heavy** (~770 g CO2/kWh, per Eskom 2024 reporting) — every kWh of mine + smelter energy carries roughly 2× the carbon of a Utah or German equivalent.
2. **Deep-shaft mining** at Bushveld depths require continuous ventilation + cooling — additional energy load.
3. **Recovery rate is <0.1%** of mined ore — every kg of ruthenium output requires moving ~1,000+ tons of ore through the platinum/palladium extraction circuit.

**Heraeus AGXX precious metals are refined in Hanau, Germany** — adding a second jurisdiction's grid CO2 (DE ~440 g/kWh) and another logistics leg before the chemistry reaches Rudolf Geretsried for formulation.

**Triple-jurisdiction supply chain for ROX:**
Bushveld Complex SA mining → Heraeus Hanau DE refining → Rudolf Geretsried DE formulation → mill.

Every step adds transportation CO2 stacked on top of the production footprint.

---

## The "self-regenerating catalyst" marketing claim

Rudolf and Heraeus market AGXX as a "self-regenerating catalyst" — true in the strict mechanistic sense (the precious metal isn't sacrificially consumed during the antimicrobial event), but the **catalyst still has to be mined, refined, and shipped to be present on the fabric in the first place**. The "self-regenerating" property reduces nothing upstream:

- Heraeus still mines every milligram of silver and ruthenium at Bushveld and refines it at Hanau.
- The full 763 kg CO2/kg-active is embedded in the product before it ever sees the mill.
- Per-garment recovery of precious metals at end-of-life is uneconomic below industrial-fleet quantities; for apparel, the precious metals end up in landfill leachate regardless.

The catalytic-regeneration mechanism is a wash-durability story, not a sustainability story.

---

## FUZE comparison

**FUZE metamaterial at <38 kg CO2/kg active.** The math:

- **Recycled e-waste feedstock** — no virgin precious-metal mining at all.
- **Liquid laser ablation** in Salt Lake City — ~30 kWh/kg active at Utah grid (~300 g CO2/kWh = ~9 kg CO2/kg active for ablation electricity).
- **Solar-capable bench** — incremental offset during high-irradiance windows.
- **No ruthenium, no Bushveld Complex routing.**

**For a brand specifying an antimicrobial finish on a 1,000,000 m/year apparel program at typical fabric weight, swapping ROX → FUZE F1 reduces the manufacturing-phase active-ingredient CO2 footprint by 95-98%** — and that's before the binder, curing, or wastewater handling deltas are counted.

## EPA / regulatory posture

- **RUCO-BAC ROX:** No US EPA registration discoverable as of 2026-05-27. Rudolf's other RUCO-BAC products (AGP / AGL) DO have verifiable EPA registrations (84189-1, 84189-2 — confirmed via PPLS PDF fetch) — ROX is absent from the Rudolf registrant's public PPLS index. Heraeus AGXX has EU Biocidal Products Regulation (BPR) approval, but BPR is a different jurisdiction and does not confer EPA legal status. Per Rudolf's own RUCO-BAC disclaimer: "antibacterial, bactericidal, germicidal" claims are NOT EPA-acceptable without product-specific registration. ROX is also NOT registered by Canada PMRA per Rudolf's admission. If ROX is shipped to a US brand and the brand makes antimicrobial claims on the finished article, the FIFRA Section 12(a)(1)(A) misbranding exposure is on the BRAND, not Rudolf — Rudolf has punted the regulatory burden downstream.
- **FUZE F1 Full Spectrum:** EPA federally registered, California EPA approved Q1 2026, OEKO-TEX Standard 100 Class I, bluesign® approved, EPA + PFAS-free across the formulation.

## Per-dimension comparison

### CO2 (cradle-to-gate, per kg treated fabric at typical dosage)

| Source | RUCO-BAC ROX | FUZE F1 |
|---|---|---|
| Active production | 1.5-2.0 g CO2/kg fabric (2 mg/kg dosage × 763 kg CO2/kg active) | <0.04 g CO2/kg fabric (1 mg/kg × <38 kg CO2/kg active) |
| Binder (acrylic polymer matrix) | 45-70 g CO2/kg fabric (12 g/kg binder) | 0 g CO2/kg fabric |
| Curing energy (160°C) | 45-75 g CO2/kg fabric | 0 g CO2/kg fabric |
| **Total** | **~95-150 g CO2/kg fabric** | **<10 g CO2/kg fabric** |

Note: ROX's lower on-fabric dosage (2 mg/kg vs SILVERPLUS / AGP at 10-12 mg/kg) means per-kg-fabric the absolute CO2 number is comparable to other silver products in spite of the ruthenium premium. The **per-kg-active** number is where the 20× difference lives — relevant when comparing chemistries on a like-for-like basis rather than at deployment dosage.

---

## Where each chemistry belongs

- **RUCO-BAC ROX** is a premium catalytic chemistry whose marketing value derives almost entirely from the "self-regenerating" wash-durability story. Brands willing to pay the precious-metal premium AND accept the triple-jurisdiction supply chain AND ship marketing claims under an unregistered US position AND lose the Canadian antimicrobial market may find a fit. Sustainability-led brands should expect tough questions on the per-kg-active CO2 footprint.
- **FUZE F1 Full Spectrum** is the right call when sustainability, regulatory, and legal need to sign off on the antimicrobial story together. Federal EPA + California EPA + OEKO-TEX + bluesign + third-party AATCC/ASTM/ISO reports + non-leaching by design + no precious-metal supply-chain exposure.
