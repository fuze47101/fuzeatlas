## Factory environmental impact — lead with this

> **Where the chemistry is made, and how it gets to the mill, dominates the cradle-to-gate footprint.**

### RUCO-BAC AGP supply chain

- **Silver feedstock** mined and refined globally — primary silver 158 kg CO2/kg refinery-gate (Aurubis EFD 2024) up to 448 kg CO2/kg ecoinvent 3.10 global supply mix.
- **AgCl synthesis + dispersion** at Rudolf Geretsried, Germany.
- **German grid mix 2024:** ~440 g CO2/kWh (Agora Energiewende / Fraunhofer ISE).
- **Outbound logistics** add trans-continental shipping CO2 for every US, Asian, or Latin American mill.

### FUZE supply chain

- **Recycled-electronics feedstock** in Salt Lake City, Utah.
- **Utah grid mix 2024:** ~300 g CO2/kWh — already 32% lower than DE.
- **Solar-capable laser ablation** can offset additional grid load during high-irradiance windows.
- **No virgin precious-metal mining** — eliminates the 158-448 kg CO2/kg upstream silver contribution entirely.

## The Rudolf EPA disclaimer — verbatim quote

From Rudolf's own RUCO-BAC series disclaimer (https://rudolf.com/uploads/rudolfgroup/Documents/disclaimer_ruco_bac_series_en.pdf):

> "EPA accepts non-public health claims as specified in PR Notice 2000-1.
> Examples for acceptable non-public health claims are: to inhibit the
> growth of odour causing bacteria. Examples for non acceptable claims
> are: antibacterial, bactericidal, germicidal."

And:

> "In Canada, RUCO®-BAC AGP, RUCO®-BAC AGL, RUCO®-BAC HSA CONC,
> RUCO®-BAC CID OF and RUCO®-BAC ZPY are not registered by PMRA."

**EPA PPLS verification 2026-05-27 — the registration exists, but it covers the wrong claim scope.** The 84189-2 master label registers AGP as "Product EP 10%" — a "Broad Spectrum Biocide for use in the manufacture of polymers, plastics, textiles, and surface coatings." That's a treated-article preservative. The label expressly states: "Manufactured products incorporating Product EP 10% may not make any public health claims relating to antimicrobial activity without first obtaining an EPA registration for the manufactured product which permits such claims. When incorporated into treated articles, this product does not protect users of any such treated articles or others against food borne or disease causing bacteria, viruses, germs, or other disease causing microorganisms." Yet RUCO-BAC AGP marketing routinely claims "antibacterial, antiviral, antimicrobial" protection on finished apparel — exactly the public-health claims the registration prohibits. **FIFRA Section 12(a)(1)(E) misbranding pattern** — same regulatory exposure as the IFTNA PROTX2 case.

## Per-dimension comparison

### CO2 (cradle-to-gate, per kg treated fabric)

| Source | RUCO-BAC AGP | FUZE F1 |
|---|---|---|
| Active production | 80-150 g CO2/kg fabric | <1 g CO2/kg fabric |
| Binder (acrylic co-polymer, formaldehyde-crosslinked) | 55-85 g CO2/kg fabric | 0 g CO2/kg fabric |
| Curing energy | 35-65 g CO2/kg fabric | 0 g CO2/kg fabric |
| **Total** | **170-300 g CO2/kg fabric** | **<10 g CO2/kg fabric** |

### Water, waste, VOC
- AGP: ~220 L water/kg active, formaldehyde-crosslinker residue, binder VOC at cure.
- FUZE: ~80 L water/kg active, no formaldehyde, no curing VOC.

### End-of-life + skin exposure
- AGP: silver leaches per Reed et al. ES&T 2010 + Benn & Westerhoff ES&T 2008. Brand customer cannot legally claim antimicrobial benefit in Canada per Rudolf's admission.
- FUZE: non-leaching contact-kill, federal + California EPA, anti-odor positioning via Moraxella osloensis ATCC 19976 on ISO 20743 / AATCC 100.

---

## Where each chemistry belongs

- **RUCO-BAC AGP** is verifiably EPA-registered as a treated-article biocide (84189-2). Brands willing to ship "antibacterial" finished-apparel claims that substantially differ from that registered scope AND lose the Canadian market for any antimicrobial label posture can deploy it — but the FIFRA Section 12(a)(1)(E) misbranding exposure is real.
- **FUZE F1** is the choice when sustainability, legal, and regulatory all need to sign off on the same antimicrobial story without exposure to Section 12(a)(1)(A) misbranding risk.
