# FUZE® Global Regulatory & Claims — Research Notes (June 2026)
Sourced backing for the "Global Compliance" part of the brand book. Verify with local regulatory counsel before any binding external use.

## Universal principle (all markets)
A FUZE-treated textile is a **treated article**: it may claim to protect **the article itself** (odor, freshness, resistance to mildew/deterioration), **not** to protect the **wearer** from disease. What differs by market is the registration mechanism behind the active and the exact wording each regulator accepts.

## United States — EPA / FIFRA (Treated Article Exemption)
- Treated articles are exempt from pesticide registration when the article is treated with a registered active and claims stay on protecting the article — **40 CFR 152.25(a)** / **EPA PR Notice 2000-1**.
- **FIFRA §25(j)(2)** — "safe," "non-toxic," "harmless," "non-poisonous" **false/misleading safety claims are prohibited** on pesticidal products. → The 2021 "safe / non-toxic / environmentally friendly" claims are non-compliant as written.
- Off-limits without product-specific public-health registration: "antibacterial/germicidal," kill rates ("99.9%"), named pathogens, wearer disease/virus protection, "EPA-approved/validated wash count."

## European Union — BPR (EU) 528/2012, Article 58 (Treated Articles)
- A FUZE-treated textile = "treated article." It may be placed on the EU market **only if the active substance is approved, or still under assessment in the BPR Review Programme, for the relevant product-type (PT9 — fibre/textile preservatives)**, AND the active-substance **supplier is on the ECHA Article 95 list**.
- Review-programme actives: treated articles may be marketed pending the final approval decision (subject to national law). Source: ECHA Treated Articles guidance; Reg. 528/2012.
- **Art. 58(3) labeling** (when a biocidal property is claimed): state the claim, name the active substance(s), include relevant instructions. Suppliers must answer consumer information requests **within 45 days**.
- ⚠️ **WATCH-ITEM (internal, not for the brand book):** the elemental-silver active (CAS 7440-22-4) PT9 assessment is **still ongoing — assessment report to the BPC expected ~Q4 2026**, and the Swedish evaluating authority has **proposed a CMR Cat. 1B (reproductive toxicant) classification** for silver in certain particle-size forms. Silver **zinc zeolite** (a different, specific compound) WAS approved Oct 2024 for PT2/PT7/PT9 — but that is not FUZE's active. EU market access for FUZE should be confirmed with EU counsel against (a) the live PT9 review status, (b) Article 95 listing, (c) any CLP classification outcome.
  - Sources: ECHA biocidal active substances database (silver, 100.028.301); REACH24H "BPC proposes not to approve three silver-containing active substances"; CIRS "EU approves Silver Zinc Zeolite for PT2/PT7/PT9" (Oct 2024); European Coatings "All that glitters is silver?"

## Canada — PCPA / PMRA (Treated Articles)
- PMRA guidance **"Acceptable Claims for Articles Treated with Antimicrobial Preservatives"** — issued 12 Mar 2024, reflects the **2023 PCPR amendments** (in force 5 Jun 2023).
- A treated article is **exempt from registration** if: (1) the antimicrobial preservative is itself **registered/authorized in Canada**, (2) use is limited to **protecting/preserving the article**, (3) applied per the registered rates/methods. Claims must match the preservative's approved label and be **qualified** ("antimicrobial"/"preservative" cannot stand alone).
- **PMRA-accepted textile claims (§3.4, verbatim):**
  - "This article has been treated with an antimicrobial agent to control odours."
  - "This product has been treated to resist the development of bacterial odours."
  - "Extends the useful life of the article by controlling deterioration caused by mould/mildew."
  - "This article contains a preservative to mitigate the development of odours."
- Where disease-protection confusion is possible, add the negative disclaimer: "This product does not protect users or others against bacteria, viruses, germs or other disease-causing organisms…"
- Pesticidal references may not be more prominent than other product features (§2.5). See DIR2016-01 (advertising).

## China — GB standards (performance-grade model)
- China uses a **performance-grade** model (not pesticide registration): test to a GB method, then an "antibacterial (抗菌)" grade may be labeled honestly.
  - **GB/T 20944.1** — agar diffusion (qualitative)
  - **GB/T 20944.2** — absorption (quantitative)
  - **GB/T 20944.3** — **shake-flask / oscillation (quantitative, DYNAMIC CONTACT)** → the method that rewards non-leaching contact-kill; China's analog to ASTM E2149 and FUZE's strongest method.
  - **GB/T 31713-2015** — hygienic/safety requirements for antibacterial textiles (safety layer).
  - Antiviral: **ISO 18184** used (data only).

## United Kingdom — GB BPR (HSE)
- Post-Brexit, **Great Britain** runs its own **GB Biocidal Products Regulation** (HSE), mirroring EU Art. 58 treated-article rules. **Northern Ireland** follows **EU BPR** under the Windsor Framework — plan GB and NI separately.
- ⚠️ **WATCH-ITEM:** HSE has begun issuing **non-approval** decisions under the GB Review Programme; affected antimicrobial-**treated articles** must stop being placed on the GB market by **28 September 2026**. Confirm the FUZE active's current GB status before GB entry.
- Sources: HSE GB BPR (hse.gov.uk/biocides); HSE Northern Ireland overview; REACH24H "UK HSE Issues Decision on Non-Approval of Biocidal Substances under GB BPR (effective April 2026)."

## Japan — SEK Mark (JTETC) — voluntary
- Not a pesticide regime. The **SEK Mark** (JTETC / Sengikyo, accredited ISO/IEC 17065) is the recognized antibacterial / deodorant / antiviral textile credential, earned via **JIS L 1902** testing (antiviral via JIS L 1922 / ISO 18184).
- Categories: Antibacterial & Deodorant; Antimicrobial (制菌); Antiviral. Claims tied to the mark category; no disease protection.
- Sources: JTETC SEK Mark certification standards (sengikyo.or.jp); JIS L 1902.

## Korea — K-BPR (MOE)
- **Consumer Chemical Products and Biocides Safety Act (K-BPR)**, Ministry of Environment, in force 2019, modeled on EU BPR; explicitly covers **biocide-treated articles**. The FUZE active must be **approved/notified under K-BPR** for the product-type; treated textiles carry K-BPR labeling; advertising controlled.
- Antibacterial performance via **KS K 0693** (analog to JIS L 1902).
- Sources: CIRS / REACH24H K-BPR overviews; Ministry of Environment.

## Australia — APVMA (Agvet Code)
- APVMA regulates antimicrobial chemistries under the **Agvet Code**. Article-protection / "bacteriostatic" claims (protecting the textile) generally do **not** require product registration; claims to protect **people** (sanitiser / hygiene / disease) trigger **APVMA** registration and/or **TGA** (therapeutic goods).
- ❓ Less codified publicly than US/EU — confirm classification & any registration trigger with APVMA / Australian counsel before claims.
- Sources: APVMA "chemicals and products we regulate" / Agvet Code (apvma.gov.au).
